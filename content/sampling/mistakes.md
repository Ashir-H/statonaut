### Seven ways to lose a mark

- **Rounding a rejected number into range.** 89 is not 'nearly 60'. Out of range means read the next number.
- **Reading single digits when the labels are two digits.** The block width follows the labels, not the way the table is printed.
- **Forgetting to reject a repeat.** A member already chosen cannot be chosen twice.
- **Taking the first in-range member as a systematic start.** The start must be within the first interval, not just inside the population.
- **Stopping a stratified question after the proportions.** The proportions are the first mark. Then you still have to select.
- **A sample that does not add up.** The group sizes must total the sample size asked for.
- **'Just ask more people'.** A biased method stays biased however large the sample.
