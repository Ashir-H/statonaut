# Also worth knowing

## The words we use

The mode, the median and the mean are the three **measures of central
tendency**, and that is the phrase the syllabus uses whenever it means any of
the three. In everyday speech "average" almost always means the mean, which is
why the longer phrase is worth getting used to: a question asking for a measure
of central tendency is not asking for the mean in particular.

## The range

The range is the largest value minus the smallest. It describes how spread out
the data is rather than where its centre lies, so it is **not** a measure of
central tendency.

It appears in the same questions as the three measures often enough to be worth
knowing well. What it is not worth doing is offering the range when a question
has asked for a measure of central tendency.

## What comes later

Everything here is for a list of values or an ungrouped frequency table, where
the median sits at the $\frac{n+1}{2}$ th value.

Grouped data works differently and uses $\frac{n}{2}$ instead. There you will
meet estimating the mean from class mid-points, naming the modal class, and
reading the median off a cumulative frequency graph. All of those are examined,
and they belong to the grouped frequency distributions unit rather than here.
