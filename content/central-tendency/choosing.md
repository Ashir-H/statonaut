# Choosing a measure

## What the question requires

A question of this kind asks which measure of central tendency best describes a
set of data, and why. Both parts are required: a measure named without a reason
is an incomplete answer, and the reason carries a mark of its own.

The reason must refer to the data in front of it. A general claim about a
measure — that the mean is more accurate, or that the median is easier to find
— describes the measure rather than the data, and earns nothing.

## What makes a reason creditable

Credited reasons fall into four kinds.

**An extreme value.** Where one value lies far from the others it raises or
lowers the mean, while the median, which depends only on position, is
unaffected. A reason of this kind names the value responsible.

**The shape of the distribution.** Where the values are closely grouped, with
nothing far from the rest, the mean sits near the centre and no measure is
distorted.

**A comparison drawn from the data.** A figure quoted from the data settles the
matter: that a mean of 36.7 exceeds five of the six values recorded says more
than any general statement about means.

**What the measure uses or ignores.** The mean uses every value. The median uses
only the position of the values, not their size. The mode uses only the value
that occurs most often and ignores the rest.

## When each measure is preferred

The **mean** is preferred where the values are closely grouped and every value
is to be taken into account.

The **median** is preferred where one or more values lie far from the rest, so
that the mean would describe no part of the data well.

The **mode** is required where the data is not numerical, since neither a total
nor a middle position has any meaning for categories, and it is preferred where
the question concerns which value or category occurs most often.

A further case arises where a distribution has an open final class, such as
"5 or more". The mean cannot be calculated, because the values in that class
are unknown; the median can still be found if its position falls below the open
class.

## Where marks are lost

Naming a measure and offering no reason.

Offering a reason that describes the measure rather than the data.

Recommending the mean where one value lies far from the others.

Naming the mode where no value occurs more often than any other.
